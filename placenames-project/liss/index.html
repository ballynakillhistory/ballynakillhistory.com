Liss

Liss in Irish “Lios an Choirthe” meaning ringfort enclosure. In the Electoral division of Drumkeary it is 292.20 acres or 118.25 hectares in size.

In the 1841 census there were 83 people, 11 inhabited and 2 uninhabited houses in Liss and in the 1851 census there were 59 people, 8 inhabited and 2 uninhabited houses. The 1840 map indicated 22 houses or outhouses and the 1847-64 Griffith’s Valuation showed 9 houses, 13 landholders and a National School house. In the 1901 census there were 7 residences and 33 inhabitants and in the 1911 census there were 8 residences and 32 inhabitants.

As well as a National School House in the south of the townland, local research indicates there was a corn mill and a lime kiln in the east of Liss. Towards the centre there is a ringfort that the townland gets its name from and nearby there is a marble (or marl) watering hole and a wooded area reputed to be the site of an old Hedge School.
