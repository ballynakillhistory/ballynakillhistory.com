Cuilleendaeagh

Cuilleendaeagh in Irish “Coillín Dá Fhiach” meaning little wood of the two ravens is in the Electoral division of Ballynagar. It is 81.81 acres or 33.11 hectares in size.

A unique feature of this townland is an old graveyard in the south, historically referred to as Ballynagar graveyard. There are historical accounts of a man called Mullins living in a dwelling in the graveyard (marked as “Hermitage” on the historic Ordnance Survey maps), living a life of solitude, penitence and prayer. We recorded the name “Mullins’ Grotto” for this graveyard.

The 1841 census show 41 people and 6 houses. In the 1851 census there were 21 people, 6 inhabited houses and 3 uninhabited houses. The 1840 map shows 7 houses or outbuildings. In the 1847-64 Griffith’s Valuation there were 3 houses, 2 landholders and a graveyard. There were no houses recorded on the 1901 or 1911 census.

Cuilleendaeagh borders Ballynagar, Cregg, Garraunphaudeen, Gortnakilla, Knockadrum and Lecarrow North.
