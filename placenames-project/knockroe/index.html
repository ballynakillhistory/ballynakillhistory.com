Knockroe

Knockroe in Irish “An Cnoc Rua” meaning the red hill. In the Electoral division of Drumkeary it is 44.49 acres or 18 hectares in size.

In the 1841 census there were 33 people and 4 houses in the townland and in the 1851 census it was down to 19 people and 3 houses. The 1840 map indicated there were 8 houses or outbuildings. The 1847-64 Griffith’s Valuation had 2 houses and 2 landholders in the townland. In the 1901 census there are 2 residences and 4 inhabitants and in the 1911 census there are 2 residences and 12 inhabitants.

This townland has two renowned archeological monuments. In the north west there is the site of a standing stone recorded as Carrickbreaga in Irish “Carraig Bréaga” meaning the “stone of the lies” or “false rock” because of its resemblance to a human form when viewed from a distance especially at dusk time or in the moonlight.

In the centre of the townland is an unclassified megalithic tomb recorded as Leaba Diarmuida or Dermot’s bed.
